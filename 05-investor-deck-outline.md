# 05 — Investor Deck Outline

**Goal: turn the feasibility work into a pitch that answers investors' real questions in order.**

This is the slide-by-slide structure of the deck I pitched to private investors, generalised. The logic: concept → proof of market → economics → ask. Every claim on a slide must trace back to the market study (`01`) or the financial model (`03`).

---

## The 60-second version

If you only remember one thing: an investor deck for a restaurant answers five questions, in this order. **What is it? Why will it work here? Who does it? Does the math work? What do you need from me?** Everything else is supporting evidence.

## Slide-by-slide skeleton (~20 slides)

### Opening

1. **Cover** — name, one-line concept ("The first [offer] × [format] in [city]"), visual identity already present. The cover sells the ambition level.
2. **Table of contents** — investors skip around; help them.

### Section 1 · Concept

3. **The inspiration(s)** — the proven concepts (local or abroad) your idea derives from, and precisely what you are importing vs. adapting. Two validated pillars, one new combination.
4. **The place & customer experience** — what walking in feels like; the 3–4 pillars of the experience (format, atmosphere, interaction with the kitchen/bar…).
5. **Identity & art direction** — name, visual world, the details people will photograph. For a hospitality concept this is a *business* slide, not decoration.
6. **The menu architecture** — families of the offer, the signature items, what carries the margin. Not the full menu: the *structure*.
7. **Consumption moments** — how the offer flexes across lunch / dinner / other dayparts, with different formats and price points per moment.

### Section 2 · Market

8. **Market signals** — the recent openings and successes that *validate* each dynamic you build on ("each of these players proves X; none combines X and Y").
9. **Structural trends** — the 3–4 forces reshaping your segment, each backed by named examples.
10. **Competitive landscape** — the grid from the market study, condensed: who plays where, at what price, and the white space you occupy.

### Section 3 · Execution

11. **Location strategy** — district scoring from the market study: saturation, rents, customer profile, your priority zones.
12. **Team** — who runs it day-to-day, FTE count, and why this team can execute *this* concept. Include advisors/partners if they fill your gaps.
13. **Vision & scalability** — what years 3–5 look like if it works (second site? format replication?). Reference local players who scaled the same way.

### Section 4 · Business plan

14. **Revenue model** — the revenue equation: covers × basket × dayparts × weekly pattern, with the ramp-up assumption stated openly.
15. **5-year revenue forecast** — one chart, assumptions visible.
16. **5-year EBITDA forecast** — one chart, margin % on it.
17. **Investment plan** — total needs broken into capex / fees / working capital & cash reserve, and the funding structure (equity / bank / other).
18. **Break-even** — in revenue AND in covers/day, at several rent scenarios. This slide builds more credibility than any other financial slide.

### Closing

19. **Investment thesis — 5 reasons** — the recap slide investors photograph: market window, differentiation, unit economics, team, timing.
20. **Next steps & timeline** — dated milestones from today to opening; what the money unlocks at each stage.
21. **Thank you / contact.**

## Rules that served me well

- **One idea per slide**, stated in the title as a full sentence ("The market is underserved in districts X and Y", not "Market analysis").
- **Numbers in the deck = numbers in the model.** Investors will open the Excel; any mismatch costs you the meeting.
- **State estimates as estimates.** "Tickets are estimates as of [date], to be confirmed by mystery visits" builds more trust than false precision.
- **The appendix is your friend** — detailed competitor grids, full P&L and sensitivity tables go after the thank-you slide, ready for the Q&A.
