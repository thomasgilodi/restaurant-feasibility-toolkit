# 01 - Market & Competition Study Framework

**Goal: decide whether a specific market can sustain your concept - with evidence, not intuition.**

This is the structure of the full market study I ran for my own venture (15 competitors scored, 8 districts analysed). A restaurant fails or succeeds at the intersection of a zone, a concept and a cost structure; this framework covers the market side. Do it *before* falling in love with a specific location.

---

## Step 0 - Study your reference concepts first

Before analysing competitors, document the concepts that inspire yours — including abroad. For each reference: what exactly do they do (offer, format, price point), what makes it work, and which elements transfer to your market vs. which don't. Put the reference concept as line 1 of your competitor grid with threat = 0: it forces you to articulate what you are actually importing.

## Step 1 - Map the market by segment

Break the relevant market into 3–5 segments and characterise each:

| Segment | Key players | Maturity | Price range | Trend |
|---------|------------|----------|-------------|-------|
| *e.g. casual / street-food* | | growing / mature / declining | | |
| *e.g. premium-casual* | | | | |
| *e.g. fine dining / tasting menu* | | | | |

Then write down the **3–4 structural trends** shaping the segment (new formats, sourcing expectations, consumption habits, recent openings that validate demand). Each trend should either support your concept or be flagged as a risk.

**The killer question at this stage:** *how many competitors combine the same pillars as my concept?* If the answer is zero, you may have found white space — or a combination nobody wants. The rest of the study decides which.

## Step 2 - Build the competitor grid

List every relevant establishment (aim for 12–20, including indirect competitors and out-of-zone references). Columns that proved useful:

| # | Name | District | Address | Type | Format | Covers (est.) | Avg ticket | Unit price* | Positioning | Opened | Strengths | Weaknesses | Threat (1–5) |
|---|------|----------|---------|------|--------|---------------|------------|-------------|-------------|--------|-----------|------------|--------------|

*\*Unit price = the comparable base item in your category (a dish, a skewer, a roll…). It reveals positioning faster than avg ticket.*

**How to fill it honestly:** menus are public — read all of them. Visit the direct competitors as a customer (peak hour). Note covers, turns, queue, staffing. Estimated figures are fine **if you say so**: date every estimate and plan to confirm by mystery visits.

## Step 3 - Score the geography

Analyse each candidate district on the same grid:

| District | Zone | # direct competitors | Saturation | Est. rent (€/m²/yr) | Customer profile | Potential (1–5) | Recommendation |
|----------|------|----------------------|------------|---------------------|------------------|-----------------|----------------|

Use an explicit saturation scale — **HEAVILY SATURATED / SATURATED / MODERATE / UNDERSERVED / HEAVILY UNDERSERVED** — and force a one-line recommendation per district ("avoid unless…", "possible if…", "excellent — priority 1"). Rank the top 2 priorities: the best districts usually combine low direct competition, the right customer profile, and rents your model can absorb (see `03-financial-model.xlsx`).

## Step 4 - Benchmark pricing

Extract a dedicated pricing table from the competitor grid:

| Concept | Type | Lunch ticket | Dinner ticket | Unit price | Tasting/set menu | Positioning |
|---------|------|--------------|---------------|------------|------------------|-------------|

Then position yourself: the sweet spot is usually a **gap between two established price points** ("between X's casual offer and Y's premium one"). Define your target lunch ticket, dinner ticket and optional premium offer — these numbers feed directly into the financial model.

## Step 5 - SWOT & synthesis

Close with a classic SWOT — but every weakness and threat must map to a mitigation in your recommendations, or it's decoration. The synthesis page answers, in one paragraph each: is there a window of opportunity, where, at what positioning, and what would kill it.

## Step 6 - Recommendations & next steps

End the study with decisions, not observations: priority location(s), positioning & pricing, format & capacity, differentiation pillars, and a dated list of next steps (qualitative validation interviews, location scouting, chef/team search, supplier sourcing, investor file). If the study can't produce this page, it isn't finished.

## Step 7 - Sources & methodology (yes, really)

List your sources (guides, press, official sites, field visits) and state your method and its limits: *"average tickets and capacities are estimates as of [date]; to be confirmed by mystery visits."* This page is what makes the difference between research and opinion — investors notice.

---

## Go / no-go

- **Clear window + priority district identified** → proceed to `02-concept-positioning.md` and plug pricing into `03-financial-model.xlsx`
- **Window exists but weak criteria** → proceed only if they are fixable (price point, format, district)
- **No window** → walk away. The lease you don't sign is the cheapest decision you'll ever make.
