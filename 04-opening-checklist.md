# 04 - On-the-Ground Feasibility & Opening Checklist

**Goal: confront the plan with reality - location, lease, licences, suppliers, money, timeline.**

This is where desk research ends and shoe leather begins. Everything below comes from doing the work: visiting locations, calling suppliers, meeting contractors, and structuring funding. French regulatory references; adapt to your market.

---

## 1. Location scouting - scoring grid

Visit every candidate location at least twice (peak + off-peak). Score 1-5:

| Criterion | Weight | Site A | Site B | Site C |
|-----------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Footfall quality at *my* service hours | 20% | | | |
| Visibility & street presence | 10% | | | |
| Surface & layout (kitchen feasible? extraction duct?) | 15% | | | |
| Terrace potential (huge revenue lever) | 10% | | | |
| Rent ≤ 10% of realistic revenue | 20% | | | |
| Condition / cost of works needed | 15% | | | |
| Neighbourhood trend | 10% | | | |
| **Weighted total** | 100% | | | |

> **Two dealbreakers to check before anything else:** (1) an **extraction duct** (conduit d'extraction) - installing one in a building that lacks it can be impossible or cost €50k+; (2) the building's **règlement de copropriété** may prohibit restaurant use entirely.

## 2. Lease essentials (France)

- **Bail commercial 3-6-9:** 9-year commercial lease, tenant can exit every 3 years. Check the *destination* clause covers restaurant use.
- **Droit au bail / fonds de commerce:** buying an existing lease or business can cost from tens of thousands to several hundred thousand € in prime Paris - budget it in the opening budget, and remember it's negotiable.
- **Rent structure:** headline rent + charges + property tax often passed to tenant (check!). Negotiate a rent-free period (franchise de loyer) to cover works.
- **Guarantees:** expect a personal guarantee and/or 3–6 months deposit as a first-time operator.

## 3. Licences, permits & compliance

| Item | Needed for | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Licence III / IV | Alcohol service | IV (spirits) is a scarce, tradeable asset; III (wine/beer) is easier |
| Permis d'exploitation | Any alcohol licence | Mandatory 2.5-day training |
| HACCP hygiene training | Food service | At least one team member certified |
| Terrace permit (autorisation d'occupation) | Outdoor seating | City-issued, annual fee, can take months |
| ERP compliance | Opening to public | Safety & accessibility rules; verify before works, not after |
| Déclaration en mairie | Opening | 15+ days before |

## 4. Suppliers & contractors

**Method that worked for me:** never one quote - always three. For food suppliers, negotiate *after* showing projected volumes from the financial model.

- Food: 2–3 suppliers per category (produce, meat/fish, dry goods) + a backup. Compare on price, minimum order, delivery days, payment terms (30 days is standard, negotiate it).
- Beverage: wine merchants often lend equipment / offer listing incentives — ask.
- Works: architect or contractor with **restaurant references** only (extraction, cold rooms, and health-compliance mistakes are expensive). Add a 15–20% contingency on every works quote.
- Equipment: second-hand professional kitchen equipment (auctions, liquidations) can cut the equipment budget by 30–50%.

## 5. Funding structure

Typical structure for a first restaurant (France) - model the exact mix in `03-financial-model.xlsx`:

| Source | Typical share | Notes |
|--------|--------------|-------|
| Founder equity + love money | 30–40% | Banks won't lend without meaningful skin in the game |
| Bank loan | 40–60% | 7-year term is common; expect a personal guarantee; BPI can co-guarantee |
| Investor equity | 0–30% | Price it carefully — giving away 40% before opening is hard to undo |
| Subsidies / honour loans | small | Réseau Entreprendre, Initiative France: €10–50k interest-free, strong signal to banks |

> **Rule of thumb:** raise for the opening budget **plus working capital and 6 months of runway**. Most restaurant failures are not bad concepts - they are good concepts that ran out of cash in month 5.

Three financing details that are worth modelling properly (all built into `03-financial-model.xlsx`):

- **Working capital (BFR):** stock rotation days, customer credit days (near zero in restaurants) and supplier credit days. Restaurants often have *negative* working capital - customers pay cash, suppliers at 20–30 days - which helps, but the opening months still need a cash buffer.
- **Loan structure:** negotiate an **interest-only deferral period** (différé) of 6–12 months so capital repayments start after the ramp-up, not during it. Model the full amortisation table — the interest line in year 1 vs year 5 changes your P&L meaningfully.
- **Rent scenarios:** run your break-even at several rent levels *before* choosing a district. A location at +€2,000/month of rent can need 10+ more covers per day, every day, forever. The model's break-even tab does exactly this.

## 6. Timeline - T-minus 9 months

| When | Milestone |
|------|-----------|

## Final gate - the three questions

Before signing anything, answer in writing:

1. **Does the zone want this concept?** (backed by `01` + `02`)
2. **Does the model make money at realistic - not hopeful - volumes?** (backed by `03`, stress-tested at –20% revenue)
3. **Do I have opening budget + 6 months of runway secured?** (backed by `03` + section 5)

Three yeses → go. Anything else → not yet.
